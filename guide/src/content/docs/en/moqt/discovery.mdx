A subscriber does not have to know track names in advance. It can watch a
namespace prefix and let the hub tell it which namespaces exist under it,
as publishers come and go. This page runs one watcher on the prefix
`guide` and four publisher announcements: one before the watcher, two
after it, and one the server's policy refuses. Then one publisher leaves.

<Example id="moqt-discovery" />

## How it works

- The sample negotiates draft-ietf-moq-transport-22 through the
  WebTransport subprotocol `moqt-22` (the server sets
  `opt.wt_protocols`). Each side opens its own unidirectional control
  stream and sends SETUP (9.1) on it, and the hub serves requests only
  after both SETUPs have arrived.
- Each request then goes on a fresh bidi stream the client opens
  (draft-ietf-moq-transport-22 6.4.2), and the hub answers on that stream
  through `stream_reply_open`. Both namespace requests stay in force after
  their answer, until the stream is cancelled (6.4.2.3) or the session
  ends. NAMESPACE and NAMESPACE_DONE are pushed on the SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE
  stream itself.
- PUBLISH_NAMESPACE (9.14, 4.1) announces a Track Namespace (`guide/lobby`,
  `guide/room-a`, ...), and the hub answers REQUEST_OK.
- SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE (9.15, 4.2) names a Track Namespace Prefix. After
  REQUEST_OK the hub sends one NAMESPACE (9.16) for every namespace
  already published under the prefix (the initial set, here `lobby`),
  then one more each time a matching namespace is published. Each carries
  only the suffix: the fields after the prefix.
- `hub.authorize_namespace` sees every PUBLISH_NAMESPACE and
  SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE (its `msg_type` says which) with the namespace and
  the presented AUTHORIZATION TOKEN. This sample refuses any namespace
  ending in `secret`: the publisher gets REQUEST_ERROR UNAUTHORIZED (1)
  and the watcher is never told about it.
- When room-a closes its session, `wired_moqt_on_session_close`
  withdraws every namespace it published, and the hub sends NAMESPACE_DONE
  (9.17) to each watcher that was told about it.
- The client never sleeps to wait for the hub: each publisher waits for
  its own REQUEST_OK, and the watcher reads the NAMESPACE or
  NAMESPACE_DONE the hub pushes, so every step is ordered by messages on
  the wire. The hub's tick (`wired_moqt_tick` from `on_step`) retries a
  push the transport refused.
